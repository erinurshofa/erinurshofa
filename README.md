## 📊 Case Study — E-SANKEM

### Digitalizing the Death Assistance Process

One project that significantly shaped the way I work was **E-SANKEM**, a digital system for the death-assistance process.

### The problem

Before digitalization:

- submissions were handled manually,
- documents moved between organizational levels,
- status was difficult to monitor,
- people had to coordinate across different stages,
- the process could take around **three months**.

### My approach

I did **not** start by coding.

I first mapped the workflow and clarified:

- who was involved,
- what each role was responsible for,
- what happened when data was wrong,
- what happened when a submission was rejected,
- who processed the next stage,
- and what the output of each stage should be.

Only after the process was sufficiently understood did implementation begin.

### An important design decision

I also initiated a **service-duration analysis dashboard** so the organization could evaluate whether the digital process was actually improving service time.

Early implementation data showed:

| Period | Average service duration |
|---|---:|
| June | **11.18 days** |
| July | **3.72 days** |

June reflected an adaptation period while users were learning the system.

The lesson was bigger than the dashboard:

> **A system should not only run. When the project allows it, it should help people understand whether the process is getting better.**

## 🔍 My Discovery Framework

Before development, I try to answer:

### 1. People
Who is involved?

### 2. Process
How does the work happen today?

### 3. Pain
Where is the biggest bottleneck?

### 4. Data
What enters, changes, moves, and comes out?

### 5. Edge Cases
What happens when something goes wrong?

### 6. Priority
What is **required**, what is **important**, and what is merely **nice to have**?

### 7. Success
How will we know the solution actually helped?

This approach helps turn vague requests into clearer engineering decisions.
